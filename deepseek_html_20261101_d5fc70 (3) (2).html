Frequently asked questions

Color theory, conversion math, and accessibility thresholds — explained.

Harmony comes from predictable relationships on the color wheel. Analogous palettes use hues within 30–60° of each other for calm, cohesive moods. Complementary palettes pair hues 180° apart for maximum tension and contrast. Triadic and tetradic schemes space hues evenly (120° / 90°) for vibrant, balanced variety. Our generator combines one of these hue relationships with a controlled lightness ladder, so palettes stay usable across UI surfaces — not just pretty swatches.

A HEX code is just three base-16 byte values: #3B82F6 becomes R=0x3B (59), G=0x82 (130), B=0xF6 (246). RGB is that same triplet in decimal. To reach HSL, normalize each channel to 0–1, compute the max and min, then derive lightness as the midpoint, saturation from the delta, and hue from which channel dominated. Every conversion in this tool runs that math locally in under a millisecond — no server round trip, no rounding drift.

WCAG 2.1 sets four thresholds. For normal body text you need 4.5:1 for AA and 7:1 for AAA. For large text — 18pt regular or 14pt bold and above — the bar drops to 3:1 for AA and 4.5:1 for AAA. The ratio is calculated from relative luminance, which linearizes each sRGB channel before weighting it (0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B). Our checker updates all four grades in real time as you adjust either color.
